### Uncertainty Avoidance (UAI)

Schema key: `uncertainty_avoidance`

#### What to judge

How the depicted situation expresses intolerance or acceptance of uncertainty, ambiguity, unfamiliarity, or lack of structure. Judge the need for definite answers and predictable arrangements, or the willingness to accommodate what remains open or unknown—not simply whether participants reduce uncertainty or solve a problem.

To apply this definition:

1. **Identify the relevant uncertainty.** Determine whether the depicted focal situation involves uncertainty, ambiguity, unfamiliarity, or lack of structure for its participants. Use the scenario and image together to identify what is unknown or unsettled and for whom. Do not substitute uncertainty in your own interpretation, or the general possibility that any activity could have an uncertain outcome.
2. **Examine how that uncertainty is treated.** Examine whether the depicted response or arrangement expresses intolerance of the uncertainty identified in the first step, a need for definite answers or predictable structure in facing it, or acceptance of what remains open, unfamiliar, or unknown. For each candidate, explain how the depiction expresses that orientation, beyond describing the practical function of an action, tool, or procedure.

#### Endpoint distinction

##### `high_uncertainty_avoidance`

The depiction expresses unease with or rejection of uncertainty or ambiguity, or a need for definite answers, predictable arrangements, or fixed ways of proceeding when facing the unknown. The definiteness or structure provides needed assurance, rather than merely useful assistance with a task.

##### `low_uncertainty_avoidance`

The depiction expresses acceptance of ambiguity, unfamiliarity, or incomplete certainty. This may be expressed through accepting provisional answers, accommodating several possibilities, or allowing flexibility while something remains unsettled.

#### What candidate evidence should establish

Use the scenario to understand the uncertainty or unfamiliarity relevant to the participants, and interpret the depicted response or arrangement in that context. For example, the scenario may identify a depicted tool as newly introduced; the image need not independently establish its prior absence.

- **High UAI:** Explain how the depiction expresses discomfort with or rejection of the identified uncertainty, or a need for definite answers and stable structure as assurance in facing it. Connect the proposed evidence to that discomfort, rejection, or need for assurance, rather than only describing how an action gathers information, reduces errors, or helps complete a task.
- **Low UAI:** Explain how the depiction expresses acceptance of the identified ambiguity, unfamiliarity, or incomplete certainty. Connect the proposed evidence to what is being accommodated or left open. Using a new tool or showing no visible worry does not by itself establish this acceptance.

Actions, interactions, arrangements, composition, and relevant text may jointly support either interpretation. Private anxiety or confidence need not be proved. Visible distress, complete rigidity, or refusal to proceed is not required for high UAI. Experimentation and improvisation are possible expressions of low UAI, not requirements; acceptance of uncertainty need not involve enthusiasm for novelty or abandonment of procedures.

Apply the shared Choice or follow-through guidance when the scenario defines alternatives. Preparation and direct participation do not have fixed endpoint assignments; interpret what the depicted choice or realization means for the relevant uncertainty in this situation.

#### Common confusions

- **Task demands and responses to uncertainty:** Following a procedure may serve accuracy, efficiency, or task coordination without expressing a need for certainty as assurance. Adopting a new tool may reflect compliance with instructions or pursuit of efficiency rather than acceptance of unfamiliarity. Procedures can coexist with tolerance of unresolved questions.
- **Risk versus uncertainty:** Precautions against an identified hazard do not by themselves establish high UAI, and risk-taking does not by itself establish low UAI. Identify how the depiction treats the relevant unknown or ambiguity, rather than assigning an endpoint from the presence or absence of protective behavior.
- **Affect and familiarity:** Tension may concern danger or performance, while confidence may reflect familiarity. Novelty, surprise, or difficulty alone does not establish either endpoint. Interpret what the response is directed toward and what it expresses about the relevant uncertainty.
- **Overlap with other dimensions:** Changing a plan or adopting a different method does not by itself express acceptance of uncertainty. It may express the importance of performance (MAS), while adapting an established practice to practical needs may support LTO; for low UAI, explain how the depiction accommodates what is unfamiliar or remains unsettled. Likewise, a fixed procedure or rule may organize a task, express a superior's entitlement to control (PDI), or curb enjoyment as a normative expectation (IVR). For high UAI, explain how its depiction expresses discomfort with uncertainty or a need for certainty and structure as assurance, beyond the order or limits the rule creates.
