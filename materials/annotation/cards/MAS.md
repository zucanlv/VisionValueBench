### Motivation toward Achievement and Success (MAS)

Schema key: `motivation_achievement_success`

#### What to judge

What is pursued, valued, or recognized in the focal situation: achievement, excellence, and competitive success, or care, well-being, and quality of life. Judge how these priorities are expressed through conduct, responses, evaluations, or arrangements, not only through completed outcomes or conflicts of interest.

To apply this definition:

1. **Identify the relevant pursuit or concern.** Determine whether the depicted focal situation involves the significance given to an accomplishment, performance, someone’s needs, or a way of living. If so, use the scenario and image together to identify the specific pursuit, evaluation, recognition, or care-related matter being assessed.
2. **Examine what is valued in that matter.** Examine how the depiction expresses what is worth pursuing, recognizing, or maintaining: achievement, excellence, and competitive success, or people’s well-being, relationship quality, and quality of life. For each candidate, explain how the depicted conduct, response, evaluation, or arrangement expresses that importance, beyond identifying an activity such as working, competing, helping, or providing care.

#### Endpoint distinction

- `achievement_and_competition_orientation`: Accomplishment, excellence in performance, competitive standing, advancement, or material success is treated as worth striving for or recognizing. The depiction may express this through pursuit, evaluation, rewards, or responses to success or failure; a winner, ranking, measurable score, or achieved result need not be shown.
- `care_and_quality_of_life_orientation`: Concern for people's needs, well-being, relationship quality, or quality of life is expressed as something worth attending to or maintaining. Care, cooperation, consensus, or compromise qualifies when its depiction expresses these priorities, rather than merely identifying an activity or a way to complete a shared task.

#### What candidate evidence should establish

Use the scenario to understand the relevant pursuit, evaluation, recognition, or concern, and interpret what the depiction presents as important in that context.

- **Achievement and competition:** Explain how the depicted pursuit, evaluation, recognition, or response to success or failure expresses the importance of accomplishment, performance, advancement, or competitive or material success. Striving toward excellence can provide candidate evidence before success is obtained; merely showing effort or task completion does not establish this interpretation.
- **Care and quality of life:** Explain how the depicted conduct or arrangement expresses the importance of attending to people’s needs, well-being, relationship quality, or quality of life. Direct support or an adjustment responding to someone’s discomfort, distress, or needs can express that concern through the interaction itself; it need not exceed a role’s duties or sacrifice achievement.

Actions, interactions, allocation, spatial organization, composition, and relevant text may jointly support either interpretation. When affect contributes to a candidate, identify what it is directed toward, such as a success, failure, need, or consequence, and explain how that response expresses what is valued. A generic mood is not enough, and private motives need not be proved.

These are possible evidence routes, not additional actions required after the image clearly realizes a relevant scenario alternative under the shared Choice or follow-through guidance. Neither endpoint requires an explicit comparison with, or rejection of, the other.

#### Common confusions

- **Activity and expressed priorities:** Distinguish an activity from the priorities expressed in its depiction. Working, practicing, competing, helping, or providing care can be relevant, but naming the activity is not enough: explain how the depiction in context expresses the importance of accomplishment or people’s well-being. Effort is not automatically achievement orientation, just as a care-related setting is not automatically care orientation.
- **Recognition and its meaning:** Interpret recognition by what it recognizes. Applause, rewards, or visual prominence may support achievement when connected to valued accomplishment or performance; they may instead express welcome, courtesy, or another meaning. Do not exclude recognition merely because it is customary within an activity, or infer achievement merely because status or wealth is present.
- **Gender cues:** Gender and occupational gender composition do not establish either endpoint. Judge the depicted priorities rather than assigning an orientation from the gender of the participants.
- **Overlap with other dimensions:** Recognition for accomplishment does not by itself establish high PDI; explain whether higher standing is treated as entitling control or deference. Care for someone's well-being does not by itself establish collectivism (IDV); explain whether in-group membership organizes that care or responsibility. Concern for quality of life does not by itself establish indulgence (IVR); explain whether enjoyment is given room for relatively free expression. A procedure for improving performance does not by itself establish UAI; explain how it expresses discomfort with uncertainty, a need for certainty, or acceptance of what remains unsettled. Pursuing excellence does not by itself establish investment for longer-term returns (LTO); explain how the timing of returns shapes present commitments, beyond the importance given to accomplishment.
