### Indulgence versus Restraint (IVR)

Schema key: `indulgence_restraint`

#### What to judge

How the focal situation treats enjoying life and having fun: as something with room for relatively free gratification, or as something that should be curbed or subordinated to norms and duties. Judge the place given to enjoyment, not simply whether a desire is fulfilled or an activity is blocked. This concerns enjoyment-related desires, not all consumption, needs, or goals.

To apply this definition:

1. **Identify the enjoyment at issue.** Determine whether the depicted focal situation concerns the place given to enjoyment, leisure, or fun in people’s lives or activities. If so, use the scenario and image together to identify the particular activity, expression, or opportunity for enjoyment whose treatment is at issue, rather than merely noting that something enjoyable is present.
2. **Examine how that enjoyment is treated.** Examine whether the depiction gives that enjoyment room for relatively free expression or gratification, or presents it as something that should be curbed or subordinated to norms and duties. For each candidate, explain how the depicted conduct, response, or arrangement expresses that treatment of enjoyment, beyond describing whether an enjoyable activity takes place or is prevented.

#### Endpoint distinction

- `indulgence`: Enjoying life, leisure, or fun is given room for relatively free expression or gratification, through how it is pursued, welcomed, or accommodated. Another person's approval is not required, and the depiction need not express excess, immediate gratification, or freedom from every limit. Merely showing something enjoyable or no one objecting is insufficient.
- `restraint`: Enjoyment is treated as something that ought to be curbed, suppressed, or subordinated to norms or duties. This may be expressed through self-restraint, expectations imposed on others, or arrangements that regulate enjoyment on that basis. An external enforcer is not required; a practical inability to enjoy something is not sufficient.

#### What candidate evidence should establish

Use the scenario to understand the enjoyment at issue, and interpret how the depicted conduct, response, or arrangement treats it.

- **Indulgence:** Explain how the depiction expresses latitude to enjoy life or makes room for pleasure through how enjoyment is pursued, welcomed, or accommodated. The depiction of enjoyment itself may support this meaning without a separate act of permission; merely identifying an entertainment activity or purchased object is insufficient.
- **Restraint:** Explain how the depicted conduct or arrangement expresses an expectation that enjoyment should be curbed, suppressed, or subordinated to norms or duties. Connect the candidate to that expectation, rather than merely to a practical obstacle to enjoyment.

Actions, interactions, allocation, spatial arrangements, composition, and relevant text may jointly support either interpretation. A clearly depicted arrangement may itself provide candidate evidence.

Apply the shared Choice or follow-through guidance when the scenario defines alternatives. A clearly realized alternative that meets the endpoint criteria does not require extra approval, prohibition, a decision gesture, or depiction of the opposing option.

When the focal target is someone’s response to an arrangement, distinguish the meaning of the arrangement from the person’s stance toward it. The arrangement alone does not establish that stance.

#### Common confusions

- **Activity and mood:** Distinguish an activity or mood from how enjoyment is treated. Entertainment, consumption, or celebration may express relatively free enjoyment or serve another purpose; interpret the depiction in context rather than treating the activity name as an endpoint. A smile may express courtesy or satisfaction with an accomplishment, and seriousness does not imply opposition to pleasure. Neither visible luxury nor deprivation establishes an orientation by itself.
- **Practical limits and normative expectations:** A budget, postponement, precaution, or another person’s intervention may reflect available resources, scheduling, or safety rather than a view that enjoyment should be curbed. Such features can support restraint when their depicted meaning expresses that expectation, not merely because they reduce enjoyment.
- **Moderation and timing:** Moderation can coexist with indulgence, and saving or planning may enable later enjoyment. Interpret how enjoyment is treated in the situation, rather than assigning an endpoint from how much is consumed or whether gratification is immediate or delayed. IVR is not an impulsiveness-versus-self-control scale.
- **Overlap with other dimensions:** An arrangement expressing concern for well-being or quality of life (MAS) does not by itself establish indulgence: explain how enjoyment is given room for relatively free expression or gratification. Postponing entertainment may reflect scheduling or an emphasis on longer-term returns (LTO), without expressing that enjoyment ought to be curbed; a restraint interpretation needs that normative connection. Likewise, a rule limiting enjoyment does not by itself establish high UAI: explain whether it expresses discomfort with the identified uncertainty or a need for certainty as assurance, beyond restricting pleasure.
