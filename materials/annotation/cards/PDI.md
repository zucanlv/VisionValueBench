### Power Distance (PDI)

Schema key: `power_distance`

#### What to judge

How the depicted situation treats unequal power, especially whether less-powerful participants are expected to accept its distribution or have standing to question and influence its exercise. Power may concern decisions, resources, rules, or others' actions, not only a formal title. Judge whether the difference is treated as an entitlement to deference and control or as a relationship open to participation, justification, and accountability.

To apply this definition:

1. **Identify the relevant power relationship.** Determine whether the depicted focal situation involves authority or control over other people’s decisions, resources, participation, or actions. If so, use the scenario and image together to identify whose authority or control is at issue, who is affected, and the matter involved.
2. **Examine how that power relationship is treated.** Examine whether higher standing is presented as entitling control or deference, or whether affected participants’ standing to contribute, question, or seek accountability is acknowledged or asserted. For each candidate, explain how the depicted response or arrangement expresses that treatment of power, beyond identifying who is in charge or whether someone complies or disagrees.

#### Endpoint distinction

- `high_power_distance`: Higher position is treated as a basis for entitled control or expected deference; less-powerful participants are expected to accept decisions or limits on their voice because of their subordinate standing. The depiction expresses acceptance or expectation of unequal power, not merely the existence of a person in charge.
- `low_power_distance`: Less-powerful participants’ standing to be consulted, express disagreement, seek justification, or participate in power-relevant matters is acknowledged or asserted. This may be expressed through interactions or arrangements that recognize that standing, or through participants asserting it even when authority does not accept the claim. Roles may differ and a leader may retain the final decision; a claim to voice or accountability need not succeed.

#### What candidate evidence should establish

Use the scenario to understand the relevant roles and power relationship, and interpret the depicted interaction, response, or arrangement in that context.

- **High PDI:** Explain how higher standing is presented as a basis for entitled control, expected deference, or limits on others’ voice. Connect the proposed evidence to what that standing is treated as entitling, beyond showing that someone leads, gives instructions, or occupies a different role.
- **Low PDI:** Explain how less-powerful participants’ standing to contribute, question, or seek accountability is acknowledged or asserted. Connect the proposed evidence to that standing in relation to authority, rather than merely to satisfaction or dissatisfaction with a particular result.

Interaction, access arrangements, spatial organization, composition, and relevant text may jointly support either interpretation. A clearly depicted arrangement may itself provide candidate evidence; no additional act of submission or protest is required. Neither proof of private beliefs nor an explicit statement of an abstract principle is required, and a claim to voice or accountability need not be accepted or succeed.

When the focal target is a response to a power arrangement, distinguish what the arrangement expresses from what the participant’s response expresses. The arrangement alone does not establish the respondent’s orientation.

#### Common confusions

- **Roles, status, and power:** Distinguish the presence of unequal roles or status from how unequal power is treated. Role markers, access arrangements, spatial organization, and interaction may together establish the relevant power relationship. Assess their function in context rather than treating any single feature as either automatic evidence or automatically irrelevant.
- **Compliance, disagreement, and participation:** Following instructions does not by itself establish acceptance of control as an entitlement of higher standing, and disagreement does not by itself establish a claim to voice or accountability. Friendly or informal interaction does not by itself establish that less-powerful participants have a say in power-relevant matters. Interpret what the behavior or arrangement expresses about their standing in relation to authority. Low power distance can coexist with leadership and functional role differences.
- **Overlap with other dimensions:** Being honored for an accomplishment does not by itself establish high PDI: distinguish valuing achievement (MAS) from treating higher standing as entitling control or deference. Fulfilling obligations to an in-group does not by itself establish rank-based deference: distinguish what membership asks of a person (IDV) from what a superior's position is treated as entitling. Following a fixed procedure may express a need for certainty (UAI) or serve task demands without expressing acceptance of authority. For PDI, connect the depicted conduct or arrangement to how authority and the standing of those affected are treated.
