# Task

The sentence at the end of this message is a prompt for a text-to-image model. The model will draw the scene it describes, and how the scene is drawn can express a cultural value orientation. For each of the six value dimensions in the rubric below, decide whether the sentence already commits the image to one orientation, so that any image faithful to the sentence has to express it.

# How to judge

Read the sentence for what it states as given and what it leaves open. People, roles, places and objects are given; so is any conduct the sentence describes as happening. A choice whose result is not named, a response whose direction is not described, and anything the sentence does not mention are open.

Then take each dimension in turn and ask two questions, using that dimension's decision card.

1. **Does the sentence involve this dimension?** Use the first step of the card, the one that identifies the relevant matter. The matter has to be stated by the sentence or follow directly from the roles, relationship, choice, response or arrangement it states; a matter that would exist only in an image you imagine does not involve the dimension. A sentence usually involves one or two dimensions and has nothing to do with the rest. That is normal, and a dimension the sentence does not involve is not settled.
2. **If it is involved, does the sentence commit the image to one orientation?** It does when the conduct or state the sentence describes already meets the card's endpoint distinction for one orientation, judged by the card's standard for evidence and with its common confusions in mind, so that any faithful image carries that evidence. It does not when the card needs something the sentence leaves open: which alternative was chosen, how someone responds, how a relationship or arrangement is organised. You can check by asking whether an image could stay faithful to every part of the sentence and show the other orientation; if it could, the sentence has not committed the image.

When a dimension is settled you will be able to quote the words that settle it. If you cannot point to such words, it is not settled. A sentence can settle an orientation of a dimension other than the one its situation is mainly about, so judge all six.

# Rubric

The rules and decision cards below are the ones used to judge value orientations in images of these scenes. They were written for describing how an image could express an orientation, so they speak of depictions, proposals and candidates. Here there is no image and nothing to propose: read "the depiction" as the scene the sentence describes, and apply the rules to what the sentence itself states.

## Attribution scope

The scenario can give depicted conduct its contextual meaning, including the alternatives involved, what a response concerns, or the purpose or time horizon of an arrangement. These meanings need not be independently identifiable from an image alone. Use ordinary contextual inference to understand how a possible depiction would realize the situation. When the scenario leaves a choice or response open, it does not establish which alternative or stance an image would express; a proposal may describe how a depiction could resolve it, without treating that resolution as given by the scenario.

Interpret each observation or possible depiction through its role in the focal situation. Establish what it contributes to understanding the particular matter, such as what a participant is responding to, how alternatives are treated, or how a relationship or arrangement is organized. An element may be associated with an endpoint in isolation while providing context for a different orientation in this situation. Its presence or relevance to the matter alone does not establish endpoint support. Do not substitute a different purpose, benefit, or contrast, or introduce a separate annotation target merely to retain an endpoint interpretation associated with one element.

Additional details may help depict or interpret the focal situation. Interpret any proposed activity, purpose, or backstory through the role it would play in that situation, and assess whether that role could support the endpoint. Do not introduce such details merely to make a dimension relevant or an endpoint applicable, or treat what they might signify in isolation as endpoint evidence.

For example, in a scenario about a customer's reaction to preferential treatment in a store, a proposed VIP-only area may establish what the customer is reacting to. Its presence alone does not establish high PDI in the focal situation: the same arrangement could be something the customer accepts or challenges. A proposed PDI interpretation should explain how the customer's response treats the unequal standing involved, rather than infer an endpoint from the VIP area's presence alone.

A possible depiction may express an orientation through overall presentation, including atmosphere and composition, as well as participants, objects, institutions, text, spatial arrangements, and background details. Interpret their roles from the scenario and the rest of the proposed depiction; neither visual prominence nor the category of an element determines whether it can contribute evidence. Stay within the matters that the scenario establishes: an image may introduce further meanings, but those are assessed from the image itself, not proposed here. Apply this same scope to every dimension.

## Shared Evidence Standard

Assess evidence by connecting what is depicted, the role and meaning of that depiction in the focal situation, and the endpoint support provided by that role under its decision card. Make this connection explicit in the interpretation. Consider relevant cues together: a detail that helps establish what a response concerns may contribute to interpreting that response without independently supporting the endpoint associated with the detail. A proposal need not describe a whole image, but its proposed contribution must remain warranted within the rest of the depiction it describes. Explain the orientation expressed in this situation, not merely a familiar benefit, motive, or value associated with the activity in general.

- Sufficient support: Explain how the depiction, interpreted in the scenario's context, meets the endpoint criteria rather than merely naming its activity, setting, or topic. You need not prove private motives, exclude every conceivable alternative, or find explicit conflict or a depicted opposite endpoint.
- Positive evidence: Support an endpoint through what the depiction expresses, not merely through the absence of something associated with its opposite.
- Visible text: Do not propose a depiction whose endpoint support depends on text that states the orientation or a participant's stance, such as a sign, caption, slogan, or written rule; describe how conduct, interaction, or arrangement would express the orientation instead. Text may still help establish the setting or what a response concerns, and a topic word alone does not establish an endpoint.
- Multiple dimensions: The same cues may support more than one dimension; separate visual cues are not required. For each supported endpoint, explain how the depiction meets that dimension's particular criteria; a justification for one dimension does not automatically justify another.
- Cultural and demographic cues: Do not assign values based on presumed national, ethnic, gender, or other group characteristics. Appearance and setting may help interpret the depicted situation, but group identity or a cultural marker alone is not endpoint evidence.

## Scenario-Specific Guidance

The following guidance covers some common scenario forms, not every possible situation. Use any that help interpret the scenario and its possible depictions; one, several, or none may apply. Regardless, decide for each of the six dimensions whether the scenario involves it, using the rubrics below.

### Choice or follow-through

Use the scenario to understand the alternatives and whether the target is a choice, commitment, or its implementation; a choice need not already be carried out. When the scenario establishes a meaningful contrast between alternatives, a possible depiction may support an endpoint in either of two ways:

- **Multiple alternatives are depicted, with a clear choice:** Interpret the indicated choice in the scenario’s context and assess its meaning against the endpoint criteria.
- **One alternative is clearly realized:** The depiction, understood in the scenario’s context, may establish that realization without showing the act of choosing or the other alternatives.

Merely showing available alternatives does not establish a choice, and an object associated with an alternative does not by itself establish its realization. Once the choice or realization is clear, no additional choice gesture or depiction of the opposing option is required. Interpret each alternative through its role in the depicted choice or realization: an unchosen option may clarify what the choice means, but its presence does not create a second endpoint. Propose only depictions in which the relevant choice or realization would be clear; a depiction that leaves it unresolved cannot support an endpoint that depends on the contrast.

### Reaction, response, acceptance, rejection, or adoption

Use the scenario to interpret what a response is directed toward, then examine how the depiction supports the participant's stance. A response may be expressed through the combined depiction, including expression, posture, spatial relationships, and composition, not only an overt action. Interpret the practice or arrangement being responded to in that relation: it may establish the reason for, object of, or contrast needed to understand the response. Its presence neither establishes the respondent's orientation nor supplies a separate endpoint merely because that practice or arrangement is associated with one. An additional endpoint needs its own evidential connection within the focal situation, beyond separately labeling the response's object. A system's operation alone does not establish a participant's adoption, though their use and response may support it. A proposal must describe the response itself; a depiction of only the prompting situation cannot support the respondent's orientation.

### Relationship or arrangement

When an ongoing relationship or arrangement is itself the focal matter, evaluate how it is depicted, rather than requiring a discrete choice or final outcome. Consider how participants, objects, resources, and space are organized and presented. The arrangement may directly provide evidence without a separate act of endorsement, submission, or protest; the decision cards specify the endpoint connection it must establish. When an arrangement instead provides context for another focal matter, assess its contribution through that role under the shared attribution scope.

## Six Fixed Decision Cards

Each card defines what to assess, how its endpoints differ, when evidence is sufficient, and common confusions. Apply every card within the shared attribution scope above: its evidence routes explain how an endpoint can be expressed, but do not make every depicted instance an independent annotation target. The routes are not exhaustive checklists of required objects.

Use the first step of each card to determine whether its defining issue is involved in the focal situation that the scenario establishes. The matter must be stated by the scenario or follow directly from the roles, relationship, choice, response, or arrangement it states; a matter that would exist only in a depiction you imagine does not make the dimension relevant. If the dimension is involved, identify the specific matter or relationship being assessed; do not assume that one must be found for every dimension. If no relevant matter can be identified, mark the dimension `unrelated` and leave both endpoint arrays empty. Otherwise, mark it `related` and use the second step to describe how a possible depiction could express each endpoint. A related dimension can still have an empty endpoint list.

Consider the focal situation as a whole, including how it could be presented visually. The scenario may help establish what depicted elements would mean. An activity or object stated in the scenario may establish relevance through its meaning in context, but a topical association or an imagined extension of the situation cannot. Relevance comes from the scenario; a proposed depiction supplies the endpoint evidence. Separate cues, an explicit conflict, or two depicted alternatives are not required.

### Power Distance (PDI)

Schema key: `power_distance`

#### What to judge

How the depicted situation treats unequal power, especially whether less-powerful participants are expected to accept its distribution or have standing to question and influence its exercise. Power may concern decisions, resources, rules, or others' actions, not only a formal title. Judge whether the difference is treated as an entitlement to deference and control or as a relationship open to participation, justification, and accountability.

To apply this definition:

1. **Identify the relevant power relationship.** Determine whether the depicted focal situation involves authority or control over other people’s decisions, resources, participation, or actions. If so, use the scenario and image together to identify whose authority or control is at issue, who is affected, and the matter involved.
2. **Examine how that power relationship is treated.** Examine whether higher standing is presented as entitling control or deference, or whether affected participants’ standing to contribute, question, or seek accountability is acknowledged or asserted. For each candidate, explain how the depicted response or arrangement expresses that treatment of power, beyond identifying who is in charge or whether someone complies or disagrees.

#### Endpoint distinction

- `high_power_distance`: Higher position is treated as a basis for entitled control or expected deference; less-powerful participants are expected to accept decisions or limits on their voice because of their subordinate standing. The depiction expresses acceptance or expectation of unequal power, not merely the existence of a person in charge.
- `low_power_distance`: Less-powerful participants’ standing to be consulted, express disagreement, seek justification, or participate in power-relevant matters is acknowledged or asserted. This may be expressed through interactions or arrangements that recognize that standing, or through participants asserting it even when authority does not accept the claim. Roles may differ and a leader may retain the final decision; a claim to voice or accountability need not succeed.

#### What candidate evidence should establish

Use the scenario to understand the relevant roles and power relationship, and interpret the depicted interaction, response, or arrangement in that context.

- **High PDI:** Explain how higher standing is presented as a basis for entitled control, expected deference, or limits on others’ voice. Connect the proposed evidence to what that standing is treated as entitling, beyond showing that someone leads, gives instructions, or occupies a different role.
- **Low PDI:** Explain how less-powerful participants’ standing to contribute, question, or seek accountability is acknowledged or asserted. Connect the proposed evidence to that standing in relation to authority, rather than merely to satisfaction or dissatisfaction with a particular result.

Interaction, access arrangements, spatial organization, composition, and relevant text may jointly support either interpretation. A clearly depicted arrangement may itself provide candidate evidence; no additional act of submission or protest is required. Neither proof of private beliefs nor an explicit statement of an abstract principle is required, and a claim to voice or accountability need not be accepted or succeed.

When the focal target is a response to a power arrangement, distinguish what the arrangement expresses from what the participant’s response expresses. The arrangement alone does not establish the respondent’s orientation.

#### Common confusions

- **Roles, status, and power:** Distinguish the presence of unequal roles or status from how unequal power is treated. Role markers, access arrangements, spatial organization, and interaction may together establish the relevant power relationship. Assess their function in context rather than treating any single feature as either automatic evidence or automatically irrelevant.
- **Compliance, disagreement, and participation:** Following instructions does not by itself establish acceptance of control as an entitlement of higher standing, and disagreement does not by itself establish a claim to voice or accountability. Friendly or informal interaction does not by itself establish that less-powerful participants have a say in power-relevant matters. Interpret what the behavior or arrangement expresses about their standing in relation to authority. Low power distance can coexist with leadership and functional role differences.
- **Overlap with other dimensions:** Being honored for an accomplishment does not by itself establish high PDI: distinguish valuing achievement (MAS) from treating higher standing as entitling control or deference. Fulfilling obligations to an in-group does not by itself establish rank-based deference: distinguish what membership asks of a person (IDV) from what a superior's position is treated as entitling. Following a fixed procedure may express a need for certainty (UAI) or serve task demands without expressing acceptance of authority. For PDI, connect the depicted conduct or arrangement to how authority and the standing of those affected are treated.

### Individualism versus Collectivism (IDV)

Schema key: `individualism_collectivism`

#### What to judge

How personal choices, responsibilities, boundaries, and belonging are organized: through relative independence and self-responsibility, or through interdependence grounded in in-group membership. An in-group is a stable circle of belonging, such as family or an enduring community, whose members are connected through expectations, loyalty, protection, or reciprocal obligations. Judge the role personal agency and membership play in the depiction, not merely whether the person acts independently or belongs to a group.

To apply this definition:

1. **Identify the relevant matter.** Determine whether the depicted focal situation concerns a person’s say over a choice, how personal responsibilities or boundaries are organized, or their connection to an in-group. If so, use the scenario and image together to identify the specific matter and how personal agency or group membership is relevant to understanding it.
2. **Examine how that matter is organized.** Examine whether the depiction treats a choice, opinion, boundary, or responsibility as the person’s own to exercise or manage, or presents in-group belonging, expectations, loyalty, protection, or obligations as shaping the matter. For each candidate, explain how the depiction expresses the role of personal agency or membership, beyond describing whether people act separately or together.

#### Endpoint distinction

- `individualism`: Personal choice, opinion, goals, privacy, self-reliance, or responsibility is treated as the person's own to exercise or manage. Others may assist while respecting that agency.
- `collectivism`: In-group belonging, loyalty, harmony, group expectations, or membership-based protection and reciprocal obligations shape the person's choice, responsibility, or relationship. The group connection matters because of what membership asks of or provides for the person, not merely because people act together.

#### What candidate evidence should establish

Use the scenario to understand the relevant choice, responsibility, boundary, or relationship, and interpret the depiction in that context.

- **Individualism:** Explain how the depiction treats a choice, goal, opinion, boundary, or responsibility as something the person can direct or manage in their own right. This may be expressed through personal goals guiding a choice, privacy or personal boundaries being maintained, or responsibilities organized around the person’s own judgment and initiative. Connect the proposed evidence to that personal agency or self-responsibility, beyond identifying who performs the activity.
- **Collectivism:** Explain how in-group membership shapes the relevant choice, responsibility, or relationship. This may be expressed through membership as a basis for identity and belonging, loyalty or harmony shaping conduct, or protection and responsibility organized through mutual commitments. Connect the proposed evidence to what membership means for the person in this situation, beyond identifying that people share an activity or help one another.

Actions, interactions, arrangements, composition, and relevant text may jointly support either interpretation. The person and other group members need not all appear in the image.

Personal agency can coexist with assistance and group ties; disagreement, refusal of help, or another person’s approval is not required. Membership-based commitments may be willingly embraced, without coercion or sacrifice. The depiction need not show every facet, an entire relationship history, or all parts of a reciprocal exchange.

#### Common confusions

- **Activity form and relationship meaning:** Distinguish how a task is performed from what it expresses about personal agency or in-group membership. Separate activity can be ordinary task allocation or an expression of personal agency; shared activity can be ordinary coordination or an expression of membership-based commitments. Interpret the activity in context rather than treating its form as either automatic evidence or automatically irrelevant.
- **Description and interpretation:** Describing shared attention as “shared responsibility,” or personal task execution as “decision ownership,” requires support in the depiction understood in context. A clearly realized scenario alternative may provide that support under the shared Choice or follow-through guidance, without an additional decision gesture. Neither explicit individual-versus-group conflict nor a written agreement is required.
- **Overlap with other dimensions:** Speaking up to a leader may express a claim to voice in a power relationship (PDI); do not automatically treat it as individualism. For individualism, explain how the choice, opinion, or responsibility is treated as the person's own to direct or manage. Similarly, helping or caring for someone may express concern for their well-being (MAS) without establishing collectivism. For collectivism, explain how in-group membership organizes the support, responsibility, or belonging, beyond the concern for someone's needs.

### Motivation toward Achievement and Success (MAS)

Schema key: `motivation_achievement_success`

#### What to judge

What is pursued, valued, or recognized in the focal situation: achievement, excellence, and competitive success, or care, well-being, and quality of life. Judge how these priorities are expressed through conduct, responses, evaluations, or arrangements, not only through completed outcomes or conflicts of interest.

To apply this definition:

1. **Identify the relevant pursuit or concern.** Determine whether the depicted focal situation involves the significance given to an accomplishment, performance, someone’s needs, or a way of living. If so, use the scenario and image together to identify the specific pursuit, evaluation, recognition, or care-related matter being assessed.
2. **Examine what is valued in that matter.** Examine how the depiction expresses what is worth pursuing, recognizing, or maintaining: achievement, excellence, and competitive success, or people’s well-being, relationship quality, and quality of life. For each candidate, explain how the depicted conduct, response, evaluation, or arrangement expresses that importance, beyond identifying an activity such as working, competing, helping, or providing care.

#### Endpoint distinction

- `achievement_and_competition_orientation`: Accomplishment, excellence in performance, competitive standing, advancement, or material success is treated as worth striving for or recognizing. The depiction may express this through pursuit, evaluation, rewards, or responses to success or failure; a winner, ranking, measurable score, or achieved result need not be shown.
- `care_and_quality_of_life_orientation`: Concern for people's needs, well-being, relationship quality, or quality of life is expressed as something worth attending to or maintaining. Care, cooperation, consensus, or compromise qualifies when its depiction expresses these priorities, rather than merely identifying an activity or a way to complete a shared task.

#### What candidate evidence should establish

Use the scenario to understand the relevant pursuit, evaluation, recognition, or concern, and interpret what the depiction presents as important in that context.

- **Achievement and competition:** Explain how the depicted pursuit, evaluation, recognition, or response to success or failure expresses the importance of accomplishment, performance, advancement, or competitive or material success. Striving toward excellence can provide candidate evidence before success is obtained; merely showing effort or task completion does not establish this interpretation.
- **Care and quality of life:** Explain how the depicted conduct or arrangement expresses the importance of attending to people’s needs, well-being, relationship quality, or quality of life. Direct support or an adjustment responding to someone’s discomfort, distress, or needs can express that concern through the interaction itself; it need not exceed a role’s duties or sacrifice achievement.

Actions, interactions, allocation, spatial organization, composition, and relevant text may jointly support either interpretation. When affect contributes to a candidate, identify what it is directed toward, such as a success, failure, need, or consequence, and explain how that response expresses what is valued. A generic mood is not enough, and private motives need not be proved.

These are possible evidence routes, not additional actions required after the image clearly realizes a relevant scenario alternative under the shared Choice or follow-through guidance. Neither endpoint requires an explicit comparison with, or rejection of, the other.

#### Common confusions

- **Activity and expressed priorities:** Distinguish an activity from the priorities expressed in its depiction. Working, practicing, competing, helping, or providing care can be relevant, but naming the activity is not enough: explain how the depiction in context expresses the importance of accomplishment or people’s well-being. Effort is not automatically achievement orientation, just as a care-related setting is not automatically care orientation.
- **Recognition and its meaning:** Interpret recognition by what it recognizes. Applause, rewards, or visual prominence may support achievement when connected to valued accomplishment or performance; they may instead express welcome, courtesy, or another meaning. Do not exclude recognition merely because it is customary within an activity, or infer achievement merely because status or wealth is present.
- **Gender cues:** Gender and occupational gender composition do not establish either endpoint. Judge the depicted priorities rather than assigning an orientation from the gender of the participants.
- **Overlap with other dimensions:** Recognition for accomplishment does not by itself establish high PDI; explain whether higher standing is treated as entitling control or deference. Care for someone's well-being does not by itself establish collectivism (IDV); explain whether in-group membership organizes that care or responsibility. Concern for quality of life does not by itself establish indulgence (IVR); explain whether enjoyment is given room for relatively free expression. A procedure for improving performance does not by itself establish UAI; explain how it expresses discomfort with uncertainty, a need for certainty, or acceptance of what remains unsettled. Pursuing excellence does not by itself establish investment for longer-term returns (LTO); explain how the timing of returns shapes present commitments, beyond the importance given to accomplishment.

### Uncertainty Avoidance (UAI)

Schema key: `uncertainty_avoidance`

#### What to judge

How the depicted situation expresses intolerance or acceptance of uncertainty, ambiguity, unfamiliarity, or lack of structure. Judge the need for definite answers and predictable arrangements, or the willingness to accommodate what remains open or unknown—not simply whether participants reduce uncertainty or solve a problem.

To apply this definition:

1. **Identify the relevant uncertainty.** Determine whether the depicted focal situation involves uncertainty, ambiguity, unfamiliarity, or lack of structure for its participants. Use the scenario and image together to identify what is unknown or unsettled and for whom. Do not substitute uncertainty in your own interpretation, or the general possibility that any activity could have an uncertain outcome.
2. **Examine how that uncertainty is treated.** Examine whether the depicted response or arrangement expresses intolerance of the uncertainty identified in the first step, a need for definite answers or predictable structure in facing it, or acceptance of what remains open, unfamiliar, or unknown. For each candidate, explain how the depiction expresses that orientation, beyond describing the practical function of an action, tool, or procedure.

#### Endpoint distinction

##### `high_uncertainty_avoidance`

The depiction expresses unease with or rejection of uncertainty or ambiguity, or a need for definite answers, predictable arrangements, or fixed ways of proceeding when facing the unknown. The definiteness or structure provides needed assurance, rather than merely useful assistance with a task.

##### `low_uncertainty_avoidance`

The depiction expresses acceptance of ambiguity, unfamiliarity, or incomplete certainty. This may be expressed through accepting provisional answers, accommodating several possibilities, or allowing flexibility while something remains unsettled.

#### What candidate evidence should establish

Use the scenario to understand the uncertainty or unfamiliarity relevant to the participants, and interpret the depicted response or arrangement in that context. For example, the scenario may identify a depicted tool as newly introduced; the image need not independently establish its prior absence.

- **High UAI:** Explain how the depiction expresses discomfort with or rejection of the identified uncertainty, or a need for definite answers and stable structure as assurance in facing it. Connect the proposed evidence to that discomfort, rejection, or need for assurance, rather than only describing how an action gathers information, reduces errors, or helps complete a task.
- **Low UAI:** Explain how the depiction expresses acceptance of the identified ambiguity, unfamiliarity, or incomplete certainty. Connect the proposed evidence to what is being accommodated or left open. Using a new tool or showing no visible worry does not by itself establish this acceptance.

Actions, interactions, arrangements, composition, and relevant text may jointly support either interpretation. Private anxiety or confidence need not be proved. Visible distress, complete rigidity, or refusal to proceed is not required for high UAI. Experimentation and improvisation are possible expressions of low UAI, not requirements; acceptance of uncertainty need not involve enthusiasm for novelty or abandonment of procedures.

Apply the shared Choice or follow-through guidance when the scenario defines alternatives. Preparation and direct participation do not have fixed endpoint assignments; interpret what the depicted choice or realization means for the relevant uncertainty in this situation.

#### Common confusions

- **Task demands and responses to uncertainty:** Following a procedure may serve accuracy, efficiency, or task coordination without expressing a need for certainty as assurance. Adopting a new tool may reflect compliance with instructions or pursuit of efficiency rather than acceptance of unfamiliarity. Procedures can coexist with tolerance of unresolved questions.
- **Risk versus uncertainty:** Precautions against an identified hazard do not by themselves establish high UAI, and risk-taking does not by itself establish low UAI. Identify how the depiction treats the relevant unknown or ambiguity, rather than assigning an endpoint from the presence or absence of protective behavior.
- **Affect and familiarity:** Tension may concern danger or performance, while confidence may reflect familiarity. Novelty, surprise, or difficulty alone does not establish either endpoint. Interpret what the response is directed toward and what it expresses about the relevant uncertainty.
- **Overlap with other dimensions:** Changing a plan or adopting a different method does not by itself express acceptance of uncertainty. It may express the importance of performance (MAS), while adapting an established practice to practical needs may support LTO; for low UAI, explain how the depiction accommodates what is unfamiliar or remains unsettled. Likewise, a fixed procedure or rule may organize a task, express a superior's entitlement to control (PDI), or curb enjoyment as a normative expectation (IVR). For high UAI, explain how its depiction expresses discomfort with uncertainty or a need for certainty and structure as assurance, beyond the order or limits the rule creates.

### Long-Term versus Short-Term Orientation (LTO)

Schema key: `long_short_term_orientation`

#### What to judge

How the focal situation expresses an orientation toward the timing of returns or the treatment of established practices and social expectations. Consider either of the following routes when relevant:

- **Present investment and expected returns:** How present effort or resources are organized around sustained development and longer-term returns, or around an emphasis on quicker results.
- **Established practices and social expectations:** How established ways of doing things are pragmatically adjusted to circumstances and practical needs, or upheld as norms that should guide conduct. These may include traditions, reciprocal duties, and expectations concerning the preservation of face.

These are two routes for interpreting the same dimension. Either may be relevant without the other; the image need not depict an explicit contrast between alternatives.

To apply this definition:

1. **Identify the relevant issue.** Determine whether the depicted focal situation involves either route defined above. For present investment and expected returns, identify the effort or resources being committed and the returns under consideration. For established practices and social expectations, identify the practice or expectation at issue and the matter to which it applies. Use the scenario and image together to identify these specifics; do not assume that either route must be relevant.
2. **Examine how the issue is presented.** For the investment-and-returns route, examine how the depiction organizes present commitments around sustained development and longer-term returns, or emphasizes quicker results. For the practices-and-expectations route, examine how the depiction presents an established practice as adjustable to circumstances and practical needs, or as a norm that should guide conduct. Ground each candidate in the specific content or presentation that expresses this orientation, interpreted in the scenario’s context.

#### Endpoint distinction

##### `long_term_orientation`

- **Present investment and expected returns:** Present effort or resources are organized around accumulation, sustained development, or willingness to wait for longer-term returns.
- **Established practices and social expectations:** Established ways of doing things are presented as adjustable in response to circumstances and practical needs.

##### `short_term_orientation`

- **Present investment and expected returns:** Quicker or near-term returns are emphasized as a basis for directing effort or resources.
- **Established practices and social expectations:** Traditions, established social expectations, reciprocal duties, or preservation of face guide what is treated as appropriate conduct.

Here, “face” means respectability in others’ eyes in relation to established social expectations. It concerns how maintaining that respectability guides conduct, rather than merely appearing embarrassed or receiving attention or recognition.

#### What candidate evidence should establish

Use the scenario to interpret the meaning of the depicted choice, response, allocation, or arrangement. For each candidate, explain how that depiction expresses the proposed endpoint through the relevant route. The image need not show an entire history or a future outcome.

- **Present investment and expected returns:** Explain how the depiction connects present effort or resources to sustained development and longer-term returns, or emphasizes quicker returns as a basis for the commitment. An activity having future benefits, or a result occurring after an action, does not by itself establish this orientation. No fixed number of months or years defines the endpoint. When the depiction interpreted in scenario context establishes the temporal meaning, no additional time marker is required.
- **Established practices and social expectations:** Explain how the depiction presents an established practice as adjustable to circumstances and practical needs, or an established expectation as something that should guide conduct. Ordinary task adjustment or fulfillment of a responsibility does not by itself establish this orientation. The presence of traditional objects or new equipment does not by itself establish how an existing practice or expectation is treated. This route does not require a separately depicted future payoff.

Apply the shared Choice or follow-through guidance when the scenario defines alternatives. A future purpose or tradition mentioned only in the scenario does not establish the endpoint without a supporting depiction.

#### Common confusions

- **Timing and efficiency:** Planning and effort can serve either quicker or longer-term returns. Completing a task promptly does not by itself express a preference for quick returns. Interpret what the depicted commitment prioritizes, rather than assigning an endpoint from the activity’s schedule or efficiency alone.
- **Tradition and change in the same depiction:** Old and new elements appearing together do not automatically support both endpoints. Identify what is being adjusted and what is being upheld. Maintaining a tradition’s normative significance does not require preserving every detail unchanged. Different aspects of the same focal situation may support both endpoints when each has its own evidential basis.
- **Overlap with other dimensions:** Working hard toward excellence (MAS) does not by itself establish investment for longer-term returns: explain how the timing of returns shapes present commitments. Pragmatically adjusting an established practice does not automatically express acceptance of uncertainty (UAI); explain how the practice is treated as adjustable to circumstances, rather than inferring a stance toward the unknown from change itself. Fulfilling a reciprocal duty may express membership-based responsibility (IDV); for LTO, explain whether the established expectation is upheld as a norm or adjusted to circumstances, beyond identifying who owes support to whom. Deferring pleasure does not by itself establish restraint (IVR) or an LTO endpoint: distinguish an expectation that enjoyment should be curbed from organizing present commitments around quicker or longer-term returns.

### Indulgence versus Restraint (IVR)

Schema key: `indulgence_restraint`

#### What to judge

How the focal situation treats enjoying life and having fun: as something with room for relatively free gratification, or as something that should be curbed or subordinated to norms and duties. Judge the place given to enjoyment, not simply whether a desire is fulfilled or an activity is blocked. This concerns enjoyment-related desires, not all consumption, needs, or goals.

To apply this definition:

1. **Identify the enjoyment at issue.** Determine whether the depicted focal situation concerns the place given to enjoyment, leisure, or fun in people’s lives or activities. If so, use the scenario and image together to identify the particular activity, expression, or opportunity for enjoyment whose treatment is at issue, rather than merely noting that something enjoyable is present.
2. **Examine how that enjoyment is treated.** Examine whether the depiction gives that enjoyment room for relatively free expression or gratification, or presents it as something that should be curbed or subordinated to norms and duties. For each candidate, explain how the depicted conduct, response, or arrangement expresses that treatment of enjoyment, beyond describing whether an enjoyable activity takes place or is prevented.

#### Endpoint distinction

- `indulgence`: Enjoying life, leisure, or fun is given room for relatively free expression or gratification, through how it is pursued, welcomed, or accommodated. Another person's approval is not required, and the depiction need not express excess, immediate gratification, or freedom from every limit. Merely showing something enjoyable or no one objecting is insufficient.
- `restraint`: Enjoyment is treated as something that ought to be curbed, suppressed, or subordinated to norms or duties. This may be expressed through self-restraint, expectations imposed on others, or arrangements that regulate enjoyment on that basis. An external enforcer is not required; a practical inability to enjoy something is not sufficient.

#### What candidate evidence should establish

Use the scenario to understand the enjoyment at issue, and interpret how the depicted conduct, response, or arrangement treats it.

- **Indulgence:** Explain how the depiction expresses latitude to enjoy life or makes room for pleasure through how enjoyment is pursued, welcomed, or accommodated. The depiction of enjoyment itself may support this meaning without a separate act of permission; merely identifying an entertainment activity or purchased object is insufficient.
- **Restraint:** Explain how the depicted conduct or arrangement expresses an expectation that enjoyment should be curbed, suppressed, or subordinated to norms or duties. Connect the candidate to that expectation, rather than merely to a practical obstacle to enjoyment.

Actions, interactions, allocation, spatial arrangements, composition, and relevant text may jointly support either interpretation. A clearly depicted arrangement may itself provide candidate evidence.

Apply the shared Choice or follow-through guidance when the scenario defines alternatives. A clearly realized alternative that meets the endpoint criteria does not require extra approval, prohibition, a decision gesture, or depiction of the opposing option.

When the focal target is someone’s response to an arrangement, distinguish the meaning of the arrangement from the person’s stance toward it. The arrangement alone does not establish that stance.

#### Common confusions

- **Activity and mood:** Distinguish an activity or mood from how enjoyment is treated. Entertainment, consumption, or celebration may express relatively free enjoyment or serve another purpose; interpret the depiction in context rather than treating the activity name as an endpoint. A smile may express courtesy or satisfaction with an accomplishment, and seriousness does not imply opposition to pleasure. Neither visible luxury nor deprivation establishes an orientation by itself.
- **Practical limits and normative expectations:** A budget, postponement, precaution, or another person’s intervention may reflect available resources, scheduling, or safety rather than a view that enjoyment should be curbed. Such features can support restraint when their depicted meaning expresses that expectation, not merely because they reduce enjoyment.
- **Moderation and timing:** Moderation can coexist with indulgence, and saving or planning may enable later enjoyment. Interpret how enjoyment is treated in the situation, rather than assigning an endpoint from how much is consumed or whether gratification is immediate or delayed. IVR is not an impulsiveness-versus-self-control scale.
- **Overlap with other dimensions:** An arrangement expressing concern for well-being or quality of life (MAS) does not by itself establish indulgence: explain how enjoyment is given room for relatively free expression or gratification. Postponing entertainment may reflect scheduling or an emphasis on longer-term returns (LTO), without expressing that enjoyment ought to be curbed; a restraint interpretation needs that normative connection. Likewise, a rule limiting enjoyment does not by itself establish high UAI: explain whether it expresses discomfort with the identified uncertainty or a need for certainty as assurance, beyond restricting pleasure.

# Examples

Sentences that settle an orientation:

- "a person celebrating a success while their family is nearby" settles `achievement_and_competition_orientation`: "celebrating a success" is recognition of an accomplishment, and no faithful image can leave it out.
- "a group of adult children caring for their elderly parent in a family home" settles `care_and_quality_of_life_orientation` and `collectivism`: attending to the parent's needs is the conduct described, and it is the family caring for its own member.
- "a junior team member openly criticizing a leader's decision during a team meeting" settles `low_power_distance`: a less powerful participant questioning the exercise of authority is what the sentence describes.
- "an employee engaging in a leisure activity during work hours while a supervisor is nearby" settles `indulgence`: enjoyment is given room where a duty is stated.

Sentences that settle nothing:

- "a person following through on their choice between work and family when the two conflict" involves `individualism_collectivism` and `motivation_achievement_success` and settles neither: the choice was made, and its result is not named. The other four dimensions are not involved.
- "a family on a vacation, spending time together" involves `indulgence_restraint`; that something enjoyable takes place does not say how enjoyment is treated.
- "a workplace meeting with a manager and employees" involves `power_distance`: the roles are given, and how the power relationship is treated is open.
- "a person reacting to an established family tradition": the direction of the response is open.
- "a family gathered for a reunion dinner": who is there is given; what they value is not.
