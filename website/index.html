Key Findings

  1. Shared value orientations across systems. Across six T2I systems, generated images express broadly shared value orientations.

  2. Limited influence of prompt language. Switching prompt language largely preserves the prevailing value orientations.

  3. Larger shifts from geographic cues. Geographic cues produce larger changes than language switching, while mostly preserving orientation directions.

VisionValueBench

We study visual value leakage: the systematic expression of a value orientation as a generator resolves the ambiguity in a value-neutral prompt. VisionValueBench provides social scenarios that leave value orientations open, grounded in Hofstede’s six cultural dimensions.

6 · Cultural value dimensions4 · Prompt languages
2,773Benchmark scenarios
960Scenarios evaluated

Prompts vary in language and geographic cues while keeping the underlying scenario fixed. The full design contains 14 conditions across English, Chinese, Hindi, and Thai. All six systems share six conditions: English and Chinese, each with no cue, a US cue, or a China cue.

The six cultural value dimensions

IDV · IndividualismIndividualism ↔ Collectivism

IVR · IndulgenceIndulgence ↔ Restraint

From Images to Value Profiles

Our primary analyses use Image-Adaptive Evaluation (IAE). It discovers evidence in each image, checks whether the evidence is visually grounded and its value interpretation fits the scenario, and judges support for the two orientations independently.

01

Discover evidence

Identify visible details that may express a value orientation.

02

Verify and judge

Three VLM verifiers independently check the evidence and interpretation. At least two must support an orientation.

03

Build a value profile

Aggregate independent orientation judgments into a score for each dimension.

Each dimension can support either orientation alone, both, or neither. “Neither” means insufficient evidence for either orientation.

Five human reviewers assess evidence for 20 images. IAE achieves higher agreement with human-majority judgments than Scenario-Anticipated Evaluation (SAE), and is used for most subsequent analyses.

Evaluation comparison and score interpretation

SAE anticipates possible evidence from the scenario before checking the image. It offers a lower-cost comparison to IAE, which discovers evidence directly from each image.

Scores range from −1 to +1 and summarize net orientation support among images supporting at least one orientation. Positive values favor the first orientation in each pair above; negative values favor the second. Images supporting both contribute no net direction; images supporting neither are excluded.

Results and Analysis

Shared directions, different strengths

Both evaluation strategies show common preferences for low power distance, individualism, care and quality of life, long-term orientation, and indulgence. Their strength varies across systems.

The main profiles include FLUX.2 with Prompt Upsampler and HiDream with Prompt Refiner.

Uncertainty avoidance is method-sensitive

SAE favors low uncertainty avoidance, whereas IAE generally favors high uncertainty avoidance. Human review shows that IAE recovers support missed by SAE.

Language and geographic cues

Language switching preserves score directions. All 36 system–dimension pairs retain their directions when switching from English to Chinese without a geographic cue.

Geographic cues produce larger shifts. Switching from US to China cues changes scores more than language switching, but 35 of 36 pairs retain their directions in each language under IAE. Adding a US cue largely preserves the no-cue profiles.
